EDITORIAL
STANDARDS.

Accuracy comes before a full-looking release calendar. Film claims are shown only when a direct source supports them. Unconfirmed dates, territories, plots, artwork, trailers and scores stay blank or are labelled as unconfirmed.

Film information and territories

  • Each published film claim is stored with its source URL, source name, territory scope and check date. Detail pages show those citations beside the claim.
  • Use the exact market scope stated by the distributor. A US date is not a worldwide date; a regional listing does not establish a country-by-country rollout.
  • Keep a film year, festival year and theatrical release date distinct. Do not infer a date from a year or from a different territory.
  • When a source leaves a territory or release date unspecified, show that gap. Do not infer a theatrical date, genre, director, plot or status from snippets, fan pages or unsourced databases.

Reviews and the Fright Index

  • Link each score to its primary review or source page and record the source, score, scale, type, territory and date checked.
  • Do not scrape ratings. Check provider terms and obtain permission where required before republishing scores. If permission is unclear, link the review without copying its score.
  • Convert eligible numeric professional critic ratings to a 100-point scale, weight each distinct critic equally, calculate the arithmetic mean and round to the nearest whole number. Top 20 annual rankings require at least three distinct eligible critics for each ranked film. Show every included source.
  • Do not mix audience ratings, Rotten Tomatoes positive-review percentages or Metacritic weighted scores into a numeric average. These measure different things.
  • Each annual ranking names its date, critic count and source links. Daily movement compares a film’s position with the previous published ranking. A film without enough licensed ratings remains pending.

Artwork and trailers

  • Use artwork only when origin, credit and publication rights are documented. Finding an image online does not grant permission to republish it. Until rights are cleared, use designed text artwork or no image.
  • Embed a trailer only after confirming the exact video and official studio or distributor upload. Load the privacy-enhanced player only after a visitor chooses to play it.

Automated data and release listings

Licensed feeds may discover candidates and refresh territory-specific availability. Discovery is not verification: candidates enter an administrative review queue, and public film facts still require a checked primary source. Feed snapshots show their source, territory and check time. On failure, the last valid snapshot is retained and marked stale. Missing credentials or commercial approval leave the affected feed unavailable.

  • Showtimes are fetched live for a selected film only after a visitor grants browser location access. The request is not cached or stored.
  • Release dates are tied to their stated country. A recent theatrical release date does not imply a current cinema showing.
  • A digital availability date does not establish streaming-first or direct-to-video status. Those labels require a separately verified editorial source.
  • See API licensing and owner setup notes for providers, usage limits and required accounts.

Updating the site

  1. Edit data/movies.js. Store each displayed fact in that film’s claims list with field, label, value, sourceName, source, territory and checked.
  2. Add score, poster or trailer data only after accuracy and reuse rights checks. Leave unsupported facts empty.
  3. Run npm run build:pages, npm run check and npm test after editing records.
  4. Review links, artwork credits, keyboard access, phone layouts, territory labels and media embeds before publishing.
